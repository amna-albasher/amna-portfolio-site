problem: [
  "Standing up a production-grade food ordering platform on Kubernetes meant coordinating four very different concerns at once: application code, infrastructure provisioning, delivery pipelines, and chart-based deployments, without letting any one of them block the others. Most small teams handle this by bundling everything into a single repo and a single pipeline, which works until the infrastructure changes need to move faster than the application code, or vice versa, and the coupling starts causing unnecessary deployment freezes. Security scanning is also frequently the first thing cut under time pressure, becoming a manual, easy-to-skip step rather than a hard gate.",
  "Beyond the repo structure, there was a real question of how to keep the cluster observable without adding operational overhead. A Kubernetes platform that requires someone to manually check pod health or dig through logs to catch problems doesn't scale past a handful of services."
]
approach: [
  "The platform is split into four independently managed repositories: application layer, CI/CD pipelines, Terraform infrastructure, and Helm chart deployments, so each concern can move and be reviewed on its own timeline. GitOps delivery through ArgoCD means the cluster state is always a reflection of what's committed to Git, not a manual `kubectl apply` on someone's laptop."
]
